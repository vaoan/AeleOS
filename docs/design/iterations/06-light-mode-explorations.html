Four ways for space to be bright

The dark mode you like stays exactly as it is. These are only candidates for its counterpart — and each comes from a different real tradition, not a different amount of pink.

1 · The photographic negative

Astronomers spent a century reading the sky as glass plate negatives — pale field, dark stars, dark dust. It is the same nebula as your dark mode, exposed the other way. Historically real, immediately distinctive, and almost nobody uses it on the web.

2 · The celestial atlas

Not the sky — the map of it. Warm paper, indigo ink, constellation lines, one gold star. Fits AeleOS unusually well: it is a registry of who is who. Risk: charts read as scholarly, which may be too cool for a fursona community.

3 · Close to the star

The conceptual inversion: dark mode is far out in the void, light mode is near the star — corona warmth, the nebula pushed to the rim. It matches your own metaphor exactly, since AeleOS is the star the moons orbit. Warmest and friendliest of the four.

4 · Rose dust — what you have now

Kept here for comparison. The weakness you felt is real: it is the dark mode with the lights turned up, so it inherits the palette without gaining an idea of its own.

Why I think rose dust felt wrong

It is the only one of the four that is not about anything. The other three each answer "where am I?" — looking at a negative plate, reading a chart, standing near the star. Rose dust just says "same place, brighter", and that is why it reads as a washed-out version rather than a counterpart.

My pick would be 1 or 3. The negative is the most striking and the most honest to the dark mode — literally the same nebula, other exposure. Close to the star is the warmest and fits your own metaphor best. The atlas is the most beautiful idea but the coolest in tone, and this is a community of characters, not a library.
