Typography — same bones, different voice

All three keep DM Sans for body text, the same face Libra uses — that is the shared skeleton. What changes is the display face and the mono. The mono matters unusually much here: platform IDs and handles are everywhere, and /me puts a UUID on screen as a hero element.

A · Space Grotesk + DM Sans + JetBrains Mono

The display face is literally named for this. Slightly quirky letterforms — look at the a and S — so the wordmark has character without a logo. JetBrains Mono is built for distinguishing 0/O and 1/l, which is the whole job when an ID is the hero.

B · Sora + DM Sans + IBM Plex Mono

Cleaner and more neutral — geometric, even, unfussy. Reads as trustworthy infrastructure, which suits an identity provider. The trade is character: it is the safest of the three and the least memorable.

C · Bricolage Grotesque + DM Sans + Space Mono

The most expressive — tight, slightly condensed, a bit playful. Closest in spirit to Libra's neobrutalist energy while using none of its fonts. Space Mono is characterful but weaker at long strings, which is a real cost when a UUID is on screen.

Judge it on the ID, not the wordmark

Every option looks fine set in 26px. The question is the line underneath: 45242b95-3aed-5bf1-8f67-fd8c8b8c17e6. People will read that aloud, paste it into support tickets and compare it across apps. JetBrains Mono and IBM Plex Mono are engineered for exactly that; Space Mono is styled for display and gets tiring at length.

All faces are free on Google Fonts, self-hostable through next/font, so nothing here costs money or an external request at runtime.
